# Source and copyright policy

## Source order

1. Foundational papers and current primary papers.
2. Official model cards, technical reports, research pages, and code.
3. Supplied textbooks for stable exposition and notation.
4. University lecture notes and course recordings from the originating course.
5. Researcher essays for clearly labeled interpretation.
6. Forums and social media only for questions, failure reports, and leads that
   are checked against primary evidence.

Every claim record stores a source ID, status, scope, caveat, and `asOf` date
when it can drift. A source supports only the claim it actually demonstrates.

## Supplied books

- Bishop, *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning*: use for probability,
  linear models, graphical models, kernels, mixture models, neural networks, and
  approximate inference. Recompute examples and cite chapter/page.
- Welch, *The Illustrated Guide to AI*: use as a visual-learning reference and
  cross-check its analogies against primary literature. Recreate original site
  diagrams unless one specific figure is essential and separately cleared.
- Brown, Roediger, and McDaniel, *Make It Stick*: apply retrieval practice,
  spacing, interleaving, generation, and calibration to lesson design. Do not
  turn learning-science findings into universal guarantees.
- Hamming, *The Art of Doing Science and Engineering*: use for research taste,
  problem choice, communication, and long-horizon reflection, while separating
  Hamming's advice from empirical learning science.

## Copyright rules

Paraphrase by default. Quote only the smallest amount needed for analysis and
keep a source locator. Do not commit full-text extractions, page renders, or book
screenshots. A public screenshot requires a page-level rationale, rights/fair-use
review, attribution, and confirmation that an original diagram would be worse
for understanding. Algebrica's CC BY-NC material may inform the editorial
system with attribution, but its content and diagrams are not copied wholesale.

## Frontier expiry

Named systems from 2025 onward belong in dated case studies. Each one answers:

- What was actually trained and evaluated?
- Against which baseline and protocol?
- What did the result not demonstrate?
- What evidence would change the verdict?
- When should this page be refreshed?
